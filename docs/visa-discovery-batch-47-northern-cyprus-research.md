# UniGo destination discovery batch 47 — Northern Cyprus (TRNC-administered area)

Checked: 15 November 2026  
Passport scope: Bangladesh ordinary passport  
Purpose: tourism / visitor  
Research outcome: **new non-ISO sub-destination candidate; research-only; do not create or publish a visa requirement**

## Discovery and identity decision

Northern Cyprus is a distinct practical visitor sub-destination with local entry administration and a separate immigration information system. It is not an ISO 3166-1 country or territory code and must not be assigned an invented ISO-2/ISO-3 code. The current UniGo country master contains Cyprus but no separate Northern Cyprus record.

The United Nations Security Council's resolutions 541 (1983) and 550 (1984) call on states not to recognise a Cypriot state other than the Republic of Cyprus and reiterate non-recognition of the purported separate state. Accordingly, UniGo should describe this only as the **Northern Cyprus / TRNC-administered area**, linked to parent destination Cyprus, not as a separate sovereign country. Do not create a country-master row, country flag, or country-level visa record without an approved child-destination model.

## Official visitor-entry framework found

The TRNC Public Information Office's current foreigner guidance states that other nationalities need a valid national passport. It says only nationals of Nigeria and Armenia must obtain a visa in advance from a TRNC representative office. It lists the following general arrival evidence:

- Valid passport.
- GBP 85 or equivalent per intended day of stay.
- Round-trip ticket.
- Confirmed hotel reservation.
- For a family/friend visit, a full invitation letter with host contact details.
- The immigration officer decides admission and may grant up to 90 days.

The official Interior Ministry visitor brochure, which describes the Residence Permits and Visas Regulation effective 23 October 2019, gives more specific tourist categories: normally up to 30 days for a tourist staying in a hotel or other accommodation; up to 90 days may be granted when evidence of prepaid accommodation is provided; a family/relative visit may receive up to 60 days. It states a maximum of 90 days in a 180-day period and at least six months' passport validity for nationals of countries without a TRNC representative office.

**Material qualification:** the current PIO page's general 90-day ceiling does not explain the older brochure's 30-day default / 90-day prepaid-accommodation distinction. The brochure is older and neither source publishes a Bangladesh-specific nationality result. Do not publish a definitive Bangladesh arrival-visa classification or guaranteed stay until the Directorate of Immigration confirms the current rule in writing.

## eVisa route

The official Directorate of Immigration portal currently offers a Tourism Visa for holidays, cultural trips and family/friend visits. It describes an average processing time of 3–5 business days. Applicants complete a form, pay after approval, and download the eVisa when finalised.

The public portal does not expose, in its accessible guidance, the Bangladesh ordinary-passport eligibility result, required upload list, government fee amount, or whether the eVisa route is mandatory or optional for Bangladeshi visitors. The 3–5 business-day figure is an average, not a guarantee, and must not be presented as a confirmed Bangladesh processing estimate. No fee is asserted.

## Bangladesh-specific transit warning via Türkiye

The TRNC Ministry of Foreign Affairs announcement dated 25 August 2023 says third-country nationals travelling to the TRNC via Türkiye must hold a double transit visa, effective 15 August 2023. Its attached official list of 57 nationalities explicitly includes **Bangladesh**.

Türkiye's current Ministry of Foreign Affairs visa table says Bangladeshi ordinary-passport holders require a Turkish visa. It says they may obtain a single-entry, one-month eVisa only if they hold a valid Schengen, US, UK or Ireland visa/residence permit and satisfy the additional eVisa conditions. The Turkish Embassy in Dhaka's transit note describes a double transit visa as two entries within three months, with up to five days per entry.

There is a route-scope question to resolve: Türkiye's MFA FAQ says a passenger who remains inside the international airport transit area does not need a transit visa, while the TRNC notice expressly requires a double transit visa for listed third-country nationals travelling via Türkiye. Do not assume the general airport-transit exemption overrides the TRNC-specific notice. Obtain current written confirmation from the Turkish Embassy in Dhaka on whether the double-transit requirement applies to the traveller's exact airside/landside itinerary and whether a qualifying Turkish eVisa can satisfy it.

A route through the Republic of Cyprus / southern crossing is not assessed in this batch. Do not treat it as an alternative without separately checking Republic of Cyprus entry permission for a Bangladeshi ordinary passport and the current crossing rules.

## Exact blockers and next actions

1. **TRNC nationality route:** obtain written confirmation from the Directorate of Immigration whether a Bangladeshi ordinary-passport tourist may use the eVisa portal, receive permission at the port, or must use a different route.
2. **Current stay and passport rules:** confirm the current tourist stay ceiling, whether prepaid accommodation still changes the maximum from 30 to 90 days, the 90/180 calculation, and minimum passport validity for Bangladeshi ordinary passports.
3. **Türkiye transit:** obtain current written confirmation from the Turkish Embassy in Dhaka about the double-transit requirement for Bangladeshis travelling via Türkiye, including airside-only transfers and the status of a qualifying Turkish eVisa.
4. **Fees and checklist:** confirm the TRNC government fee, payment method, required eVisa documents, processing estimate applicability, photo upload/printed-photo rules, and biometrics.
5. **Data model:** decide whether UniGo supports a non-ISO child destination under Cyprus. Until then, keep this candidate in the non-ISO research ledger only.

# UniGo destination discovery batch 51 — Svalbard and Jan Mayen split research

**Checked:** 15 November 2026  
**Passport scope:** Bangladesh ordinary passport  
**Purpose:** tourism / visitor  
**Research outcome:** two distinct non-ISO child-destination records identified from the existing composite ISO `SJ` row. Keep both in the non-ISO research ledger; do not publish a standalone country-level visa requirement.

## Discovery and identity decision

The current country master has one `SJ` row named **Svalbard & Jan Mayen**. This is a composite ISO 3166-1 entry, but the two places have materially different visitor-entry and access arrangements:

- **Svalbard** is open to visitors without a Norwegian visa or residence permit. It is outside the Schengen Area, so a Bangladeshi ordinary-passport holder travelling through mainland Norway/Schengen needs a Schengen visa with at least two entries to return to mainland Norway.
- **Jan Mayen** is a restricted Norwegian island with a prior-permission regime for foreign visitors, vessel and landing coordination, and temporary access limits during major construction.

A single `visa_required` field on the combined `SJ` row would conceal these differences. Neither place should be assigned an invented child ISO code. Track both as child destinations under Norway, with the existing `SJ` composite row retained unchanged until UniGo has an approved parent-child destination model.

## Candidate 1 — Svalbard

### Bangladesh ordinary-passport entry assessment

1. The Governor of Svalbard states that Norwegian authorities do not require a visa for entry to Svalbard. A valid travel document is required; for a Bangladeshi ordinary-passport traveller this means carrying the valid passport.
2. The Governor's travel-document guidance says there is no minimum validity period beyond the date of entry, but recommends validity beyond the expected departure because document-renewal opportunities on the archipelago are limited.
3. Svalbard is part of the Kingdom of Norway but outside the Schengen Area. A foreign national who requires a Schengen visa must hold one when travelling via mainland Norway/Schengen. The Governor and UDI expressly say to request **two or more entries** so the traveller can return to mainland Norway after the Svalbard visit.
4. Bangladesh is listed in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806, the list of nationalities requiring a visa when crossing the external borders of the Member States. This supports the Schengen-visa requirement for the mainland Norway leg; it does not turn Svalbard itself into a visa-required destination.
5. The existing Norway country record already covers the Bangladesh Schengen application route. Its current source ledger identifies the Embassy of Denmark in Dhaka as representing Norway for short-stay Schengen cases and VFS Global Bangladesh as the submission channel. This is a parent-country application route, not a separate Svalbard visa application.

### Stay, fee, processing and documents

- No separate Svalbard visitor visa, eVisa, visa-on-arrival or visitor-permit fee is published in the reviewed Governor/UDI guidance.
- The reviewed sources do not publish a general tourist maximum stay for a short visit. Do not invent one.
- No separate Svalbard visitor-visa processing SLA exists in the reviewed guidance. Any Schengen application processing belongs to the parent Norway/Schengen route and its Bangladesh checklist.
- A valid passport/travel document is required for the archipelago. The Schengen leg requires the usual parent-country visa application documents and the correct two-or-more-entry request for the itinerary.
- The Governor publishes a separate Schengen application checklist for people applying from Svalbard. It is not the Bangladesh application checklist and must not replace the Denmark/VFS Bangladesh route.

### Photo and checklist status

- No separate Svalbard visitor visa photo specification was found because Svalbard itself has no visa requirement.
- If the itinerary requires a Schengen visa for mainland Norway, use the current Bangladesh-specific Schengen checklist and photo/biometric instructions for the responsible mission/VAC. Do not copy a generic Svalbard checklist into the Bangladesh application record.
- Do not mark Svalbard as having “no photo required” for the complete journey. The Schengen application may require a printed photo and biometric capture under the parent route.

### Exact publication blocker

The country master/schema has no child-destination representation. Keep the child record research-only until a parent-child data model can preserve the distinction between Svalbard's no-visa local regime and the Schengen visa required for the mainland transit/return leg. Do not alter or publish the combined `SJ` country row as if both Svalbard and Jan Mayen shared one visa rule.

## Candidate 2 — Jan Mayen

### Bangladesh ordinary-passport entry and local permission

1. The official Jan Mayen visitor site says foreign citizens who wish to visit must obtain permission from Norwegian authorities before arrival.
2. Its current operational guidance gives the following permission contacts by intended visit duration:
   - up to 24 hours: Station Commander, Jan Mayen;
   - up to one week: Chief of Police in Nordland;
   - more than one week: Norwegian Ministry of Justice and Public Security.
3. The site says every visiting boat landing must be applied for in advance and coordinated with the Station Commander. The only currently permitted visitor landing point is Kvalrossbukta. Landings can be cancelled at short notice because of weather, station capacity, resupply or safety operations.
4. Foreign vessels, and Norwegian vessels with foreign captains, must separately comply with Norwegian territorial-water entry/navigation rules. The official site directs vessels to SafeSeaNet Norway / the Norwegian Joint Headquarters Coastal Surveillance Center, with a separate application/coordination process for landing and island capacity. Vessel clearance is not the same as an individual visitor's permission.
5. The official site currently warns that extensive construction from summer 2025 through winter 2027/2028 restricts and limits arrival/access for safety reasons.
6. The Norwegian Immigration Regulations state that the Immigration Act and Regulations apply to foreign nationals' access to and stay on Jan Mayen insofar as relevant, subject to Jan Mayen-specific rules. The older 1962 regulation's former passport, visa and border-control sections were repealed effective 1 May 2022. Its remaining section 8 provides a statutory permission framework for foreign nationals' stay, including police permission up to one week if the visitor can leave by ship or aircraft and Ministry permission for longer stays. The legal text says a section 8 stay permission is fee-free.
7. Bangladesh is listed in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 as a nationality requiring a visa for the Schengen external border. A Bangladeshi traveller entering mainland Norway/Schengen as part of the itinerary therefore needs the applicable Schengen visa. The sources reviewed do not conclusively establish the exact visa treatment for every possible direct non-Schengen vessel itinerary to Jan Mayen. Do not infer a Jan Mayen visa exemption from Svalbard's separate rule.

### Access, fees, processing and documents

- Jan Mayen is not an ordinary independent-tourism destination with routine passenger transport. Official Norwegian Armed Forces material says there are no regular passenger flights; visitor access is by pre-coordinated ship/boat landing, subject to permission, capacity, weather and safety.
- The Jan Mayen visitor site requires vessel applications to state the purpose and scope of the visit; arrival/departure date and time; vessel name, nationality and port of registration; ship contact details (including MMSI/call sign); captain's name and nationality; persons on board; passenger/crew/medic counts; number going ashore; and last/next port. These are vessel-application data, not a complete passenger visa checklist.
- No separate government visitor-application fee or processing SLA is stated on the current Jan Mayen visitor site. The statutory section 8 stay permission is described as fee-free; do not assume this establishes that every vessel, landing, maritime clearance or expedition service is free.
- The site does not publish a standard Bangladesh-specific passenger document checklist, funds requirement, insurance minimum, photo specification or biometric/VAC process.
- The current visitor site warns that visits and landings may be cancelled even at short notice. A scheduled ship call or a granted landing request must not be described as guaranteed access.

### Photo and checklist status

- **Unresearched for the Bangladesh Jan Mayen permission route.**
- No fixed-size photo status is not confirmed. The absence of a photo mention on the visitor site is not proof that a photo can never be requested.
- No Bangladesh VAC photo capture or biometric procedure was identified for the local Jan Mayen permission.
- If the itinerary requires a Schengen visa for mainland Norway, follow the parent Norway Bangladesh checklist separately.

### Exact publication blocker

Obtain written confirmation from the Norwegian authorities on (1) the visa requirement for a Bangladeshi ordinary-passport holder arriving directly by vessel from outside Schengen, (2) how the Immigration Act/Regulations and Jan Mayen-specific permission interact for that itinerary, (3) the current permission authority and process during the 2025–2027/2028 construction period, and (4) whether any fee or additional passenger documents apply. Keep the local access record research-only until those details and the parent-child schema are resolved.

# Destination discovery and publication review — batch 43 / import batch 45

Checked 15 November 2026. Passport class: Bangladesh ordinary passport. Purpose: tourism/visitor.

## Ascension Island (AC)

- Parent jurisdiction: Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha, a British Overseas Territory. AC is an exceptionally reserved two-letter territory code; no alpha-3 code is assigned.
- Entry: visitor eVisa required. The AIG eVisa FAQ says all nationalities require eVisas except listed exemptions and names 13 nationalities for which AIG does not issue eVisas; Bangladesh is not on that exclusion list. This establishes eligibility to apply, not a guarantee of grant.
- Stay/entries: one entry; a single stay up to three months; cumulative visitor-eVisa time limited to three months in any 12-month period.
- Fees: GBP40 standard, GBP60 fast-track, GBP10 for applicants under 12; non-refundable government charges.
- Processing: 90% targeted within 21 days and all within six weeks; fast-track aims for 14 days. Estimates are not guarantees.
- Passport: at least six months remaining at entry and enough validity to cover the stay.
- Insurance: full medical/travel cover for treatment and evacuation to country of residence; air-evacuation cover at least GBP500,000, with GBP1 million overall cover advised.
- Photo: JPEG face image, clear face, not required to be passport quality. No pixel/mm dimensions, file-size limit, background, head measurement, age limit or printed-copy count is published. Fingerprints and a face photograph may be requested as biometrics; no VAC capture route is described.
- Conditional UK rule: AIG says nationals who require a UK visa may be required to show a multiple-entry UK visa valid for both outward and return journeys before an Ascension eVisa is issued. The UK Home Office list includes Bangladesh. UniGo records this as an AIG discretionary condition; applicants should confirm with AIG for their itinerary.
- Application: online AIG eVisa portal; each traveller applies separately. Cruise passengers should confirm whether their operator arranges entry permission.
- Sources: AIG eVisa FAQ; AIG immigration page; 2026 AIG fee schedule; AIG eVisa-system Q&A; UK Home Office Appendix Visitor: Visa National List.

## Tristan da Cunha (TA)

- Parent jurisdiction: Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha, a British Overseas Territory. TA is an exceptionally reserved two-letter territory code; no alpha-3 code is assigned.
- Entry: no visa is required for visitors, but advance permission to land from the Administrator/Island Council is mandatory and a landing stamp is placed in the passport. This is not automatic visa-free entry; UniGo classifies it as prior landing permission, not a visa.
- Application: email the Secretary to the Administrator with purpose, dates, nationality, age, preferred vessel and accommodation sought. Obtain approval before travel.
- Stay/processing: no universal maximum stay or processing SLA is published; duration is approved by the Island Council and shipping may require planning well in advance.
- Main-island landing/harbour charges: GBP30 for cruise passengers and GBP15 for yacht passengers. These are not visa fees.
- Optional outer-island fee scope: the newer 2026 visitor summary lists GBP25 for Nightingale/Inaccessible including a visit certificate; an older 2025 visiting-ships page lists GBP20 for the landing-only charge. The difference is disclosed as a date/scope caveat; confirm the applicable amount with the Administrator before booking that optional excursion.
- Visitors staying on Tristan need confirmed, fully paid return passage, adequate insurance including medical evacuation to Cape Town, and sufficient funds. Accommodation/itinerary details are requested. The Island Council may request a police certificate. Cruise passengers may need to complete an indemnity declaration before going ashore.
- Photo: no separate photo upload or fixed specification was identified in the reviewed visitor/landing guidance. This does not mean the Administrator cannot request a photograph during an individual permission review.
- Sources: Tristan da Cunha Government pages for organising a visit, visitor information/charges, visiting ships/yachts and accommodation.

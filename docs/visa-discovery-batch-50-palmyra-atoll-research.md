# Palmyra Atoll — non-ISO destination discovery

**Research date:** 15 November 2026  
**Status:** Research-only. Do not create a country-master row, standalone Bangladesh visa requirement, public API entry, or country page.  
**Candidate key:** `palmyra-atoll-us`  
**Parent:** United States; Palmyra is an incorporated U.S. territory administered as the Palmyra Atoll National Wildlife Refuge. It is also grouped within the U.S. Minor Outlying Islands ISO grouping.  
**ISO-2 / ISO-3:** None assigned to Palmyra as a separate destination. Do not invent codes.

## Scope and destination identity

This candidate is specifically the **Palmyra Atoll National Wildlife Refuge**, not the whole Pacific Islands Heritage Marine National Monument and not the other U.S. Minor Outlying Islands. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) says Palmyra is the only refuge in the monument that allows recreational access by a visiting vessel under a Special Use Permit. Most other refuge units in the monument are closed to the public or limited to management and research access.

The U.S. Department of the Interior describes Palmyra as an incorporated U.S. territory. The refuge is administered by USFWS. Country-level nationality and visitor-visa rules therefore remain attached to the existing United States record; this sidecar does not create a separate nationality or visa regime.

## Bangladesh ordinary-passport tourism assessment

### Parent-country immigration layer

The U.S. Department of State identifies B-2 or combined B-1/B-2 as the visitor visa category for tourism. Bangladesh is included in the Department's visa-bond programme, effective 21 January 2026. For an applicant otherwise eligible for B-1/B-2, the consular officer determines whether a bond is required and its amount ($10,000, $15,000, or $20,000); applicants must not pay until instructed through the official consular process.

For a visa holder who has posted a bond, the Department's published conditions require entry and exit through designated commercial-air ports of entry and exclude charter air, general aviation, land and sea ports of entry. This is a material route issue for Palmyra's recreational vessel access.

### Local refuge-access layer

USFWS describes four access categories: work/contract/volunteer with USFWS or The Nature Conservancy; scientific research under a USFWS Special Use Permit; invitation through a Nature Conservancy-sponsored donor trip; and visitation by a private recreational sailboat or motorboat.

For a private recreational vessel, the published guidance states:

- prior USFWS approval is required;
- access is limited to up to seven days;
- no more than two vessels may be present at one time and no more than six may visit in a month;
- the refuge is open to visitation only when a USFWS manager is present, typically for about six months each year, primarily in summer;
- vessels need sufficient holding capacity for all black and grey water;
- hull-bottom inspection and cleaning documentation must be dated within two weeks before departure for the refuge;
- a rodent-inspection report must be completed within 48 hours before departure;
- pets are not allowed, even if kept aboard;
- access to Cooper Island is separately arranged through The Nature Conservancy.

The official page describes the sea passage from Honolulu as approximately five to seven days. The Nature Conservancy owns and operates the only runway. The reviewed official guidance does not establish a scheduled commercial passenger flight or a generally available air-tourism route.

### Material unresolved immigration/route issue

The State Department's visa-bond notice prohibits bonded B-1/B-2 holders from using sea ports of entry. CBP separately states that persons arriving in the United States on private vessels from a foreign port must report and be inspected at a designated U.S. port of entry.

The sources reviewed do **not** establish whether a Bangladeshi traveller who first enters the United States through a commercial-air port in Hawaii, then travels on a private vessel from Honolulu to Palmyra and returns by the same route, remains compliant with the visa-bond terms; nor do they establish the CBP inspection/port-of-entry treatment of that specific domestic voyage. Do not infer that the vessel route is permitted or prohibited for a bonded traveller. Obtain written confirmation from the competent U.S. authorities before presenting it as an available Bangladesh-passport tourism route.

## Fees, processing and documents

- **USFWS permit fee:** Not stated in the reviewed public visitor guidance. Do not enter a zero fee or infer that the permit is free.
- **USFWS processing time:** Not published. Prior approval and seasonal manager availability are required; no decision SLA is stated.
- **U.S. visitor-visa fee/bond:** Inherited from the parent United States record. A visa bond is not a visa fee and is not a guarantee of visa issuance.
- **Vessel documentation:** The hull-cleaning and rodent-inspection documents and waste-holding requirements above are specifically published for the recreational-vessel route.
- **Traveller document checklist:** No complete Bangladesh-specific traveller checklist for Palmyra was found. Do not convert the vessel inspection documents into a passenger visa checklist.
- **Photo:** The USFWS visitor pages reviewed do not specify a photo for the refuge permit. The parent U.S. visitor-visa route has its own DS-160 photo rules; those must not be misrepresented as a Palmyra permit photo rule. A Palmyra-specific photo status remains unresearched, not “no fixed size published.”
- **Transit:** The parent U.S. entry and any foreign-port vessel-arrival requirements must be checked against the actual itinerary. No separate Palmyra transit exemption was found.

## Research outcome

**Research-only; not publication-ready.** There is an official, tightly controlled recreational visitor route for private vessels, but the route's compatibility with the Bangladesh visa-bond commercial-air entry/exit condition is unresolved. The USFWS fee and processing details are also not published in the reviewed guidance. No Bangladesh-specific local permit checklist or photo specification was found.

### Exact next actions

1. Ask USFWS/Palmyra Refuge management to confirm the current private-vessel Special Use Permit application channel, any fee, seasonal opening dates, passenger documents, and whether non-U.S. visitors may join a vessel after entering through a commercial-air port in Hawaii.
2. Ask CBP Honolulu and the relevant CBP authority for Palmyra to confirm the port-of-entry/inspection treatment of a domestic Honolulu–Palmyra private-vessel voyage and whether a traveller subject to a U.S. visa bond may make that voyage without breaching the bond.
3. Ask the U.S. Department of State to confirm how the commercial-air-only bond condition applies to a traveller who enters and exits through commercial-air ports but makes an internal trip to Palmyra by private vessel.
4. Keep the candidate under the United States parent and research-only until the immigration route is confirmed and a parent-child destination model is approved.

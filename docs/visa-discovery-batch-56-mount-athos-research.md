## Discovery and identity decision

Mount Athos (Agion Oros / the Holy Mountain) is a self-governing monastic peninsula in northern Greece. The Mount Athos Center's pilgrim information page describes it as part of Greece with a distinct autonomous monastic administration under the Holy Community. It is not a sovereign state and has no separate ISO 3166-1 country code.

The practical visitor route is materially different from ordinary Greece tourism: foreign visitors need a separate written Mount Athos entry permit (the `diamonitirion`) issued through the Holy Executive of the Holy Mount Athos — Pilgrims' Bureau. The permit is additional to, and does not replace, the applicable Greek/Schengen immigration permission.

Keep the Greece (GR) country record authoritative for the ordinary Bangladesh-passport Schengen visa. Track Mount Athos as a child destination only if UniGo adopts a parent-child destination model that can represent local access permits without overwriting the parent's immigration rule.

## Bangladesh ordinary-passport assessment

### Greece / Schengen immigration permission

1. The current consolidated Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 lists Bangladesh in Annex I. Under Article 3, nationals of Annex I countries must hold a visa when crossing Member States' external borders. A Bangladeshi ordinary-passport visitor travelling to Greece therefore generally needs a Schengen visa, subject to any individual exemption that applies to that traveller.
2. The Hellenic Republic Embassy in New Delhi, which states that it is also accredited to Bangladesh, says Bangladeshi nationals travelling to Greece for tourism or other purposes may apply through the Embassy's external service provider, GVC World, at the Visa Application Center in Dhaka.
3. The current GVC World Bangladesh-to-Greece portal provides a Tourism category. Its instructions require applicants to complete and print the online application, schedule an appointment, and submit in person at the Greece VAC. It says applications may be lodged up to six months before travel and postal applications are not accepted.

### Separate Mount Athos access permit

The Mount Athos Center page, under the heading “Holy Executive of the Holy Mount Athos — Pilgrims' Bureau”, states the following:

- Foreign visitors must obtain a written permit from the Pilgrims' Bureau in Thessaloniki under procedures established by the Greek Government.
- Reservations are made in advance through the Bureau. The page says reservations are made six months in advance; this is not a processing-time guarantee.
- The permit is collected personally at the Pilgrims' Office in Ouranoupoli. The visitor must appear with a passport or identity document. The page says a letter of recommendation is no longer required.
- The page describes permits for a four-day visit on specified dates and says an extension may be issued by the Mount Athos authorities in Karyes.
- The page reports daily permit allocations of ten for non-Orthodox visitors (foreigners) and 100 for Greeks and Orthodox visitors. The category wording and current quota must be reconfirmed with the Bureau before publication; do not infer an applicant's category from nationality.
- Overnight visitors must arrange accommodation with the monasteries or sketes in advance.
- Only adult men may enter. Males under 18 are permitted only when accompanied by their fathers; women are prohibited under the rule described as the “avaton”. Treat this as a strict access restriction, not a visa exemption.

The Mount Athos Center page lists a EUR 30 permit amount and a EUR 10 student amount. Other non-government visitor information gives different category-specific amounts. The current fee and any applicable applicant-category distinctions have not been reconciled directly with the Pilgrims' Bureau. Do not publish a definitive permit fee.

**Important distinction:** the `diamonitirion` is a local Mount Athos access permit. It is not a Greek visa, a Schengen visa, a visa-on-arrival facility, or permission to enter mainland Greece.
